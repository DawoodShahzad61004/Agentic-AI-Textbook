The diagnostic criteria for Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in children are outlined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th Edition (DSM-5). According to the DSM-5, a diagnosis of ASD requires the presence of persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction across multiple contexts, including deficits in verbal and nonverbal communication, and deficits in developing, maintaining, and understanding relationships. Additionally, symptoms must be present in the early developmental period, typically before the age of 3. The diagnostic criteria also include symptoms of restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities. These symptoms must cause clinically significant impairment in social, occupational, or other areas of functioning.

The diagnostic criteria for ASD in children are as follows:

1. Persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction, including:
   - Deficits in verbal and nonverbal communication
   - Deficits in developing, maintaining, and understanding relationships

2. Restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities, including:
   - Stereotyped or repetitive motor movements
   - Insistence on sameness or routine
   - Highly restricted, fixated interests
   - Hyper- or hyporeactivity to sensory input

3. Symptoms must be present in the early developmental period, typically before the age of 3.

4. Symptoms must cause clinically significant impairment in social, occupational, or other areas of functioning.

It's worth noting that the diagnostic criteria for ASD in children are subject to change and may be updated as new research becomes available.
